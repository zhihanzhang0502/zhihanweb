    Key Findings & Outputs¶

    Key Findings¶

    Pay-on-Delivery Adoption Under the Wholesale Contract¶

    Under the wholesale contract, the platform offers pay-on-delivery when:

    \[ \theta < \gamma \]

    The service is attractive when the benefit of reducing the online channel's delivery-delay disadvantage exceeds the platform's cash-flow opportunity cost.

    After adoption:

    • The wholesale, online, and offline prices increase.
    • Online demand increases while offline demand decreases.
    • The manufacturer always earns a higher profit.
    • Consumer surplus decreases.
    • Social welfare increases.

    Pay-on-Delivery Adoption Under the Agency Contract¶

    Under the agency contract, adoption requires the agreement of both the platform and the manufacturer.

    The manufacturer adopts pay-on-delivery when:

    \[ \theta< \frac{1-\alpha-\beta}{1-\beta}\gamma \]

    Adoption is more restrictive because the manufacturer bears delayed revenue realization and pays an additional service fee.

    After adoption:

    • The online retail price increases.
    • Online demand increases and offline demand decreases.
    • Total market demand increases.
    • Consumer surplus may increase or decrease depending on the severity of delivery delay.
    • Social welfare increases.

    Channel Effects¶

    Pay-on-delivery produces two important channel effects:

    • Competition mitigation effect: deferred payment increases consumers' willingness to pay online and can soften price competition.
    • Channel cannibalization effect: demand shifts from the manufacturer's offline channel toward the online channel.

    Under the agency contract, coordinated pricing can also generate a market expansion effect, causing total demand to increase.

    Distribution-Contract Choice¶

    The manufacturer's contract choice depends primarily on the agency commission rate, pay-on-delivery service fee, and delivery-delay disadvantage.

    • A high agency commission rate makes the wholesale contract more attractive.
    • A high pay-on-delivery service fee makes the manufacturer more likely to choose the wholesale contract.
    • When the service fee is low and the online delivery disadvantage is severe, pay-on-delivery can make the agency contract more attractive.
    • When the delivery disadvantage is moderate, the manufacturer may prefer the wholesale contract because the platform bears the direct financial burden of pay-on-delivery.

    Product Returns¶

    When product returns are considered:

    • Pay-on-delivery becomes more likely to be adopted because it reduces consumers' return hassle.
    • The service can increase the volume of product returns.
    • The manufacturer becomes more likely to choose the wholesale contract because the platform bears the return-handling cost.

    Different Product and Cash Discount Factors¶

    Allowing product valuation and cash payment to have different discount factors changes the adoption thresholds but does not change the main strategic mechanism.

    When the relative discount factor for cash increases, the delayed-payment benefit becomes weaker and pay-on-delivery is less likely to be adopted.

    Research Outputs¶

    • Developed equilibrium solutions for the WN, WY, AN, and AY scenarios.
    • Derived adoption conditions for pay-on-delivery under wholesale and agency contracts.
    • Characterized the competition-mitigation, channel-cannibalization, and market-expansion effects.
    • Derived equilibrium regions for distribution-contract selection.
    • Evaluated manufacturer profit, platform profit, consumer surplus, and social welfare.
    • Extended the model to incorporate product returns.
    • Tested robustness using different discount factors for product valuation and cash.
    • Contributed to a co-authored research manuscript.
