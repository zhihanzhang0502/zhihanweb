# Key Findings & Outputs

## Key Findings

### Credit-Service Provision

The platform benefits from launching the consumer credit service only when consumer preference for the service exceeds the delinquency rate:

\[
\gamma > 1-\theta
\]

When this condition is not satisfied, the additional demand generated by the service is insufficient to compensate for repayment risk.

### Credit-Service Sharing

The sharing decision depends primarily on the service fee and consumer preference.

| Service-fee condition | Sharing outcome |
|---|---|
| \(\beta \leq 1-\alpha-\theta\) | The platform does not share the credit service |
| \(1-\alpha-\theta < \beta < (1-\alpha)(1-\theta)\) | The platform shares only when consumer preference is sufficiently high |
| \(\beta \geq (1-\alpha)(1-\theta)\) | The platform is willing to share, but the seller participates only when consumer preference is sufficiently high |

A win-win sharing agreement can occur when the service fee is moderate and consumers have a strong preference for the credit service.

### Effects of Exclusive Provision

When the platform provides the service only for its own product:

- The platform's product price and demand increase.
- The third-party seller lowers its price but still loses demand and profit.
- The wholesale supplier benefits from the platform's stronger market position.
- Consumer surplus increases relative to the no-credit-service mode.

### Effects of Service Sharing

When the platform shares the service with the third-party seller:

- Retail prices in both channels increase.
- Demand shifts from the platform's product toward the seller's product.
- The platform and seller can both benefit from the sharing agreement.
- The wholesale supplier earns lower profit.
- Consumer surplus decreases relative to exclusive provision.

### Consumer Participation

The extension distinguishes between consumers who use the credit service and conservative consumers who refuse to use it.

The platform introduces the service only when the proportion of participating consumers is sufficiently high. As more consumers use the service, the region in which the platform and seller both agree to share it becomes smaller.

## Research Outputs

- Developed equilibrium solutions for the NN, IN, and II operational modes.
- Derived threshold conditions for credit-service provision and sharing.
- Conducted comparative-statics analysis of repayment rates, service fees, commissions, and consumer preferences.
- Extended the model to include consumers who choose not to use the credit service.
- Performed Mathematica-based numerical simulations and strategy-region analysis.
- Contributed to a peer-reviewed publication in *Managerial and Decision Economics*.
