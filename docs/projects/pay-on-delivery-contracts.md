# Pay-on-Delivery and Distribution Contracts

This project studies how pay-on-delivery services affect distribution contract choices on e-commerce platforms.

## Project Overview

Pay-on-delivery allows consumers to postpone payment until product receipt. This service can reduce the disadvantage of online channels caused by delivery delay, but it may also create cash-flow opportunity costs for firms.

This project develops a game-theoretic model of a dual-channel supply chain, where a manufacturer sells through an offline channel and also cooperates with an e-commerce platform online. The online channel can operate under either a wholesale contract or an agency contract. The project studies when pay-on-delivery should be adopted and how it changes the manufacturer's distribution-contract choice.

## Research Questions

- When should an e-commerce platform offer pay-on-delivery?
- When should a manufacturer adopt pay-on-delivery under an agency contract?
- How does pay-on-delivery affect online and offline channel demand?
- How does pay-on-delivery change the manufacturer's choice between wholesale and agency contracts?
- What are the implications for firm profits, consumer surplus, and social welfare?

## Methodology

The project uses analytical game-theoretic models to compare different channel structures and payment schemes. The model considers four main scenarios:

1. Wholesale contract without pay-on-delivery.
2. Wholesale contract with pay-on-delivery.
3. Agency contract without pay-on-delivery.
4. Agency contract with pay-on-delivery.

The analysis uses backward induction to derive equilibrium pricing, demand, profit, consumer surplus, and social welfare outcomes.

## Main Findings

- Pay-on-delivery can mitigate the online channel's disadvantage caused by delivery delay.
- Under a wholesale contract, the platform may offer pay-on-delivery when the consumer benefit from delayed payment outweighs the firm's cash-flow opportunity cost.
- Under an agency contract, adoption is more restrictive because the manufacturer directly bears delayed revenue realization and service-related costs.
- Pay-on-delivery can reshape the manufacturer's preference between wholesale and agency contracts.
- The service may reduce consumer surplus in some cases, but it can improve social welfare within the model.

## Extensions

The project also considers two extensions:

- Product returns in the online channel.
- Different discount factors for product valuation and cash payment.

These extensions show that the main mechanism remains robust when additional operational features are introduced.
