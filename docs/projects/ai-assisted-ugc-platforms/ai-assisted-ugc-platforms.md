# AI-Assisted Creation on Pure-Content UGC Platforms

This project studies whether pure-content UGC platforms should introduce AI-assisted creation tools and how such tools affect creators' content quality decisions.

## Project Overview

Pure-content UGC platforms rely on creators to produce engaging content and attract consumer attention. With the development of generative AI, platforms can provide AI-assisted creation tools such as automatic subtitle generation, AI script writing, one-click editing, AI visual effects, AI avatars, and personalized voices.

This project develops analytical models to study a platform's decision to launch AI-assisted tools and choose the optimal assistance intensity. The model captures the trade-off between AI-driven efficiency and creativity improvements, increased content homogeneity, and platform development costs.

## Research Questions

- Under what conditions should a pure-content UGC platform launch AI-assisted creation tools?
- How should the platform choose the optimal AI assistance intensity?
- Do creators benefit from AI-assisted tools?
- How does AI usage affect content quality, browsing volume, creator profit, platform profit, and consumer surplus?
- How do creator characteristics, such as creativity and production efficiency, affect equilibrium outcomes?

## Methodology

The project develops a sequential game-theoretic model involving a UGC platform, a content creator, and consumers.

The decision sequence is:

1. The platform decides whether to introduce AI-assisted creation tools and chooses the assistance intensity.
2. The creator chooses the production level of content.
3. Consumers decide their browsing volume.

The analysis uses backward induction and comparative statics to characterize equilibrium outcomes. The model accounts for AI-driven production efficiency gains, creativity enhancement, content homogeneity, and development costs.

## Main Findings

- AI-assisted tools can improve content creativity and production efficiency, but they may also increase content homogeneity.
- The platform should introduce AI tools when creator efficiency is low, AI-induced homogeneity is limited, or creators have strong creative ability.
- The optimal AI assistance intensity is not necessarily the level that maximizes content quality, because the platform must balance quality improvement, homogeneity risk, and development cost.
- When AI tools are introduced, content quality, consumer browsing volume, creator profit, and consumer surplus can increase under the model's adoption conditions.
- The effect of creator efficiency is nuanced: after AI introduction, higher creator efficiency may not always improve creator profit or consumer surplus.
- Creator creativity consistently improves content quality and benefits the platform, creators, and consumers.

## Extensions

The project further examines:

- Different consumer weights on creativity and production quality.
- Creator competition on the platform.
- Endogenous platform subsidies to creators.
- AI technical defects and their impact on platform and creator decisions.
