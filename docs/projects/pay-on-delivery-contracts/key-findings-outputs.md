# Key Findings & Outputs

## Key Findings

### Pay-on-Delivery Adoption

Under the wholesale contract, the platform offers pay-on-delivery when

\[
\theta<\gamma.
\]

The service is attractive when the benefit of reducing the online channel's delivery-delay disadvantage exceeds the platform's cash-flow opportunity cost.

Under the agency contract, joint adoption occurs when

\[
\theta<
\frac{1-\alpha-\beta}{1-\beta}\gamma.
\]

The condition is more restrictive because the manufacturer bears delayed revenue realization and pays an additional service fee.

### Effects on Channel Outcomes

Under both contracts, pay-on-delivery makes the online channel more attractive:

\[
D_n^Y>D_n^N,
\qquad
D_f^Y<D_f^N.
\]

Demand shifts from the offline channel toward the online channel, creating a **channel-cannibalization effect**.

Under the wholesale contract:

- wholesale, online, and offline prices increase;
- the manufacturer always earns a higher profit;
- consumer surplus decreases;
- social welfare increases.

Under the agency contract:

- the online retail price and total market demand increase;
- consumer surplus may increase or decrease;
- social welfare increases.

The agency contract produces a stronger **market-expansion effect** because the manufacturer coordinates pricing across both channels.

### Distribution-Contract Choice

Pay-on-delivery changes the relative attractiveness of wholesale and agency selling.

- A high agency commission rate makes the wholesale contract more attractive.
- A high pay-on-delivery service fee also shifts the manufacturer toward the wholesale contract.
- When the service fee is low and the online delivery disadvantage is severe, pay-on-delivery can make the agency contract more attractive.
- When the delivery disadvantage is moderate, the manufacturer may prefer the wholesale contract because the platform bears the direct cash-flow burden of the service.

Therefore, pay-on-delivery is not only a payment arrangement; it also changes channel governance and the allocation of financial risk.

### Extensions

When product returns are considered, pay-on-delivery becomes more likely to be adopted because it reduces consumers' return hassle. However, it can also increase return volume and make the wholesale contract more attractive because the platform bears the return-handling cost.

Allowing product valuation and cash payment to have different discount factors changes the adoption thresholds but does not alter the main strategic mechanisms.

## Research Outputs

- Derived equilibrium solutions for the WN, WY, AN, and AY scenarios.
- Identified pay-on-delivery adoption conditions under wholesale and agency contracts.
- Compared pricing, demand, profit, consumer-surplus, and social-welfare outcomes.
- Characterized how pay-on-delivery affects distribution-contract choice.
- Extended the model to include product returns and different discount factors.
- Conducted Mathematica-based numerical analysis and contributed to the co-authored manuscript.
