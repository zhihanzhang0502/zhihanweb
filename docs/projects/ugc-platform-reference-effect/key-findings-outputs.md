# Key Findings & Outputs

## Key Findings

### Recommendation Intensity and Content Quality

Pure-content quality increases with its recommendation intensity:

\[
\frac{\partial q^*}{\partial c^*}\geq 0.
\]

When the platform recommends pure content more intensively, the creator receives more exposure and has a stronger incentive to improve content quality.

This complementary relationship remains valid under static decisions, dynamic decisions, stochastic reference quality, and alternative nonlinear user-engagement functions.

### Dynamic Versus Static Decisions

Compared with static decision-making:

- dynamic strategies consistently produce higher pure-content quality;
- pure-content recommendation intensity may be higher or lower, depending on the initial reference quality and the stage of the decision horizon;
- dynamic decision-making increases the platform's profit;
- the content creator's profit may increase or decrease.

The platform benefits from the flexibility of adjusting recommendation intensity in response to evolving consumer expectations.

### Evolution of Reference Quality

Let \(g\) denote the long-run steady-state reference quality.

When the initial reference quality is low, \(r_0<g\):

- reference quality, content quality, and recommendation intensity increase over time;
- user engagement may decrease as the initial positive reference effect gradually weakens.

When the initial reference quality is high, \(r_0>g\):

- reference quality, content quality, and recommendation intensity decrease over time;
- user engagement may increase as the initial negative reference effect weakens.

Thus, higher content quality does not necessarily imply increasing user engagement, because users continuously update their quality expectations.

### Effects of Reference-Quality Uncertainty

As the uncertainty level \(\epsilon\) increases,

\[
\frac{\partial c^{DS*}}{\partial\epsilon}>0,
\qquad
\frac{\partial q^{DS*}}{\partial\epsilon}<0.
\]

The platform recommends pure content more intensively to manage uncertainty risk, while the creator reduces content quality after observing the platform's response.

Higher uncertainty reduces the platform's profit but increases the creator's profit. The platform bears more uncertainty risk as the first mover, while the creator benefits from a second-mover advantage and lower production costs.

### Robustness

The main conclusions remain valid under alternative nonlinear user-engagement functions, including:

- quadratic and exponential aversion to shoppable content;
- square-root and power-form effects of pure-content quality.

These extensions continue to show that stronger pure-content recommendation encourages higher content quality and that dynamic strategies improve platform profit.

## Research Outputs

- Developed static, dynamic deterministic, and dynamic stochastic game models.
- Derived optimal recommendation-intensity and content-quality strategies.
- Compared platform and creator outcomes across the CN, DN, and DS scenarios.
- Analyzed the effects of initial reference quality and reference-quality uncertainty.
- Conducted Mathematica-based numerical simulations and robustness analysis.
- Contributed to a peer-reviewed publication in the *Journal of the Operational Research Society*.
