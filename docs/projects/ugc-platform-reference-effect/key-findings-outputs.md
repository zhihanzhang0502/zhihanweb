# Key Findings & Outputs

## Key Findings

### Relationship Between Recommendation and Quality

Pure content quality increases with its recommendation intensity. When the platform recommends pure content more intensively, the creator has stronger incentives to improve content quality.

This complementary relationship remains valid under static decisions, dynamic decisions, stochastic uncertainty, and alternative nonlinear user-engagement functions.

### Dynamic Versus Static Decisions

Compared with static decision-making:

- Dynamic decision-making consistently encourages creators to produce higher-quality pure content.
- The recommendation intensity of pure content may increase or decrease depending on the initial reference quality and the stage of the decision horizon.
- Dynamic decision-making increases the UGC platform's profit.
- Dynamic decision-making may either increase or reduce the content creator's profit.

The platform benefits from the greater flexibility and first-mover advantage created by dynamic decision-making.

### Initial Reference Quality

When initial reference quality is low:

- Pure content quality and recommendation intensity increase over time.
- Reference quality gradually increases toward its steady state.
- User engagement may decline as the initial positive reference effect weakens.

When initial reference quality is high:

- Pure content quality and recommendation intensity decrease over time.
- Reference quality gradually falls toward its steady state.
- User engagement may increase as the negative reference effect weakens.

### Reference-Quality Uncertainty

As uncertainty in reference quality increases:

- The platform increases the recommendation intensity of pure content.
- The content creator reduces pure content quality.
- The platform's profit decreases.
- The content creator's profit increases.

The platform must recommend more pure content to manage uncertainty risk. After observing this response, the creator can lower quality and reduce production costs without causing a substantial loss in engagement.

### Pure and Shoppable Content Balance

The platform must balance two competing objectives:

- More pure content can improve quality, attract users, and support long-term engagement.
- More shoppable content directly increases the platform's commercial exposure and revenue.

Excessive promotion of shoppable content can reduce user engagement and weaken creators' incentives to produce high-quality content.

### Robustness

The main conclusions remain valid under four alternative nonlinear user-engagement functions:

- Quadratic aversion to shoppable content;
- Exponential aversion to shoppable content;
- Square-root effects of pure content quality;
- Power-form effects of pure content quality.

## Research Outputs

- Developed static, dynamic deterministic, and dynamic stochastic game models.
- Derived optimal recommendation-intensity and content-quality strategies.
- Characterized the optimal paths of reference quality, content quality, recommendation intensity, and user engagement.
- Compared platform and creator profits across the CN, DN, and DS scenarios.
- Conducted sensitivity analyses across thousands of parameter combinations.
- Performed Mathematica-based numerical simulations and visualized dynamic paths and steady-state outcomes.
- Tested robustness using nonlinear user-engagement functions and approximate dynamic programming.
- Contributed to a peer-reviewed publication in the *Journal of the Operational Research Society*.
